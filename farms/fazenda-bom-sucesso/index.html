The Farm Story

Fazenda Bom Sucesso is the family farm of Paulo, on the road out of Vitória do Xingu in the Brazilian Amazon — about 25 minutes from town. Paulo first met our team as a translator for the CEPOTX cooperative during our earliest visits to Altamira, when the cooperative members needed a bridge to English. Over those visits, a partnership grew — and this year Paulo offered his own land for the SunMint tree-planting program.

His family has held the land for about four years. It spans roughly 54 hectares, and he is the first to say he is still learning to run it — "with our own bare hands," as he puts it. He is deliberate about how it grows: "We are in expansion — planting, not buying land. This is enough. We are planting on the land we already have." Walking the boundary with us in September 2026, he said it plainly: "It's a small farm, but it's our pride."

The region is in its hardest drought in years — no rain on the farm since June, the pasture "not green anymore… becoming the desert." Paulo refuses to plant anything without an irrigation system, and he knows the agronomy well: he works in a store selling irrigation systems and fertilizers in the city. Cattle is still the farm's main income — "very nice to work with" — but he wants that to change: "I believe there's more to offer in the farm." His bet is on planting: açaí seedlings waiting in the nursery, bananas, and cacao between them. "We have to plant trees… to change the weather. Every year we plant more and more cacao trees."

Paulo is honest about cacao: "I am still afraid of planting cacao in a large amount… the price is not always fair, and the labor is hard and expensive." So he is cautious about scale — but he loves the tree itself: "I love to plant cacao between my bananas, so I can work and I can eat." On 2 September 2026, we planted the first 10 Cacau - Hybrid trees on his land — each GPS-pinned and photo-verified into the SunMint impact map: the beginning of turning pasture into shaded cacao agroforest.
